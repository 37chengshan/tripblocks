THE PLAN IN THE ITINERARY

Kansai / 07 days / 25 blocks

The premise

Every trip has an elephant in the room: the plan itself. TripBlocks turns the week into blocks you can move — grounded in real rooms, real tables, and a quiet second opinion.

01 / The premisePlan / in blocks

The week is not a list.
It is a shape.

A seven-day trip is a living arrangement: a late night before an early train, a splurge before a quiet afternoon, a market crawl that eats the whole morning.

TripBlocks makes those tensions visible. Move a block and the cost, the route, and the pace move with it. No generated itinerary to accept or reject — just a plan you can keep editing.

02 / The methodArrange / ground / ask

Arrange.
Ground. Ask.

Three moves, in this order. The board stays the hero; the intelligence stays beside it.

  1. 01

    Arrange

    Drag a day, a stay, a long lunch. Edit the time, place, budget, and the note that tells you why it mattered. The week recomputes quietly.

    Drag / drop / edit
  2. 02

    Ground

    Open live options from FlyAI. Real hotels, real sights, real prices, real Fliggy links — then put one on the day it belongs to.

    FlyAI / Fliggy
  3. 03

    Ask

    Wander reads the full itinerary, not just the last message. It suggests one small move, with a reason. You decide whether it enters the plan.

03 / The second opinionWander / grounded

It reads the shape of the week.

Wander is not a chat window bolted onto a planner. It receives the compacted itinerary — days, times, costs, notes — and answers inside that context.

“Day 5 is doing three things in one day. Keep Kuromon, make the castle optional, and turn the evening into one standing counter. That buys you two hours and protects the Uji morning.”
Saved conversation / demoManaged Agent · grounded

Make Day 5 lighter.

Day 5 is doing three things in one day: a market, a castle, and Dotonbori at night. I would keep Kuromon, make the castle optional, and turn the evening into one standing counter instead of a full dinner. That buys you about two hours — and protects the Uji morning.
